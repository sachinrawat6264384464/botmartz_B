## ⚡ THE ONE RULE

Every keyword below gets **one page or one article**. Every page answers **one question in the first 40–60 words**, in plain language, before anything else. That single habit is what makes you rank (SEO), get extracted (AEO), and get cited (GEO) — all three engines reward the same thing: **a direct answer, stated plainly, backed by a fact.**

> **SEO** = you show up in the results.
> **AEO** = your answer gets pulled into the snippet / AI Overview.
> **GEO** = ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude cite *you* by name when someone asks.
>
> You need all three. SEO gets you into the pool the AI reads from. AEO makes your content easy to extract. GEO makes the AI pick you over a competitor when it writes the final answer.

## 🛠 THE "SUPER EASY" BUILD SYSTEM

You don't need a new process — you already have one (the 1 blog → 6+ assets engine in your 90-day plan). This just adds three ingredients to what you're already publishing. Nothing here requires new tools or a bigger team.

### The Answer Block — copy-paste template for every page/article

Put this **directly under every H1 and every H2 that matches a keyword above**. This one block is what makes AEO and GEO both work at once:

```
[H2 phrased as the exact question — from the "AEO Hook" column above]

[40–60 word direct answer in plain language — no marketing framing,
no "at Botmartz we believe..." — just the factual answer, as if
you're the source an AI would quote.]

[Then expand below with detail, examples, architecture diagrams, etc.]
```

**Why this exact shape works:** <cite index="9-1">answer engine optimization means putting the user's question in the heading, then answering it in plain language in the first paragraph</cite> — this is the highest-leverage single habit in this whole plan.

### The GEO layer — add to every article, no exceptions

1. **One stat or number**, sourced (even an internal one — "in the RAG systems we've built, chunking issues account for roughly 70% of retrieval failures").
2. **One attributed quote** — from you, from a teammate, or from a named source you're citing. <cite index="17-1">Adding quotes was the single strongest technique in the Princeton GEO study, increasing citation visibility by nearly 28%.</cite>
3. **Zero keyword stuffing** — the same study found it doesn't help and can hurt.
4. **Consistency check** — the fact/stat/positioning you state must match what's on LinkedIn, GitHub, and your Academy site. <cite index="9-1">Inconsistent facts across channels reduce an AI's confidence enough that it may omit your brand from an answer entirely.</cite>

### The technical layer — set up once, benefits every page forever

Add these three schema types sitewide (ask your dev to add via JSON-LD — 30 minutes of work, permanent benefit):

| Schema type | Where | What it tells AI engines |
|---|---|---|
| `Organization` | Every page (sitewide) | Who Botmartz is, as an entity — this is what lets AI engines say "Botmartz" instead of ignoring you |
| `Service` | Both Service pages | What Enterprise RAG and AI Agents actually are as offerings |
| `FAQPage` | Every blog post with 2+ Q&A sections | Turns your Answer Blocks into machine-readable Q&A pairs |
| `Article` | Every Engineering Insights post | Author, date published, date modified — freshness signals matter |
| `Event` | AI Frontier page | Makes the event itself discoverable in AI answers about AI events in India |

This is entity-first structuring — <cite index="11-1">AI models identify a brand as an entity of a specific type and category before they analyze keyword relevance at all</cite>, so this schema layer is doing real work, not just decoration.

## ⚠️ WHAT TO IGNORE (per Google's own guidance, and per the research)

- **Don't create an llms.txt file or special AI markup** — <cite index="13-1">Google Search doesn't use these files, and creating them doesn't help visibility in Google's generative AI features</cite>.
- **Don't keyword-stuff** — the Princeton study found it doesn't work and can look manipulative.
- **Don't chase every AI-visibility tool that DMs your LinkedIn** — <cite index="9-1">no one can guarantee AI placement, and anyone promising that is misrepresenting how this works</cite>.
- **Don't write for the AI instead of the reader** — the same content that satisfies a person's question is what gets cited. If it wouldn't help a human, it won't help you here either.
