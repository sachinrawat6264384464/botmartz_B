## Why AI Frontier Exists

<a id="why-ai-frontier-exists"></a>

### The problem with most AI events right now

Walk into almost any AI event in India today and you'll get one of three things:

**1. The hype talk.** Someone shows you what's possible, the room applauds, and nobody leaves knowing how any of it was made. Inspiration with no traction.

**2. The academic session.** Rigorous, careful, well-cited — and completely disconnected from what shipping an AI feature at a company actually involves. Depth with no deployment.

**3. The tool demo.** A vendor walks you through their platform for ninety minutes. Useful if you're buying. Not useful if you're building.

None of these produce engineers.

### What actually changed

The last eighteen months moved the centre of gravity in AI. The interesting work is no longer "which model is best." It's:

- **Agentic loops.** Systems that plan, act, observe, and re-plan — and the loop engineering required to keep them from spiralling, stalling, or burning through your token budget.
- **Tool use and orchestration.** Models are now the reasoning layer inside larger systems. The engineering is in everything around the model: routing, retries, state, fallbacks, guardrails.
- **Memory.** Short-term context management, long-term retrieval, and the very real trade-offs between the two.
- **Evaluation.** The single biggest differentiator between a demo and a product. If you can't measure whether your system got better, you're not engineering — you're guessing with extra steps.
- **Governance and explainability.** Explainable AI went quiet for a while; it's loud again, and now it comes with regulatory teeth. Board-level concern, not an engineering footnote.

These aren't advanced topics anymore. They're the baseline. And almost nobody is teaching them together, in one room, in one day, with a laptop open.

### Why Indore

Because the talent here is real, the curiosity is real, and the access is not.

Bangalore, Hyderabad and Delhi get the events. Central India gets the livestream — six months later, if at all. AI Frontier is built on a simple premise: you shouldn't have to leave your city to be at the front of your field.

This is the first edition. It's designed to be the first of many.

## Session Deep Dives

<a id="session-deep-dives"></a>

### 🧠 Think Like an AI Engineer

**10:30 – 11:45 · 75 minutes · Promit Ray**

The framing session for the entire day. Not a history lecture, not a hype reel — a working mental model for how modern AI systems are assembled, from someone who has watched the field evolve across academia, industry and startups.

**What's covered:**

**1. Where we actually stand (brief, visual)**
A fast, honest read of what changed between last year and now. Capability, cost, context, latency, reliability. What got solved, what stubbornly didn't.

**2. From chatbots to agents — what technically changed**
The architectural shift, explained properly:
- Tool use and function calling as the hinge point
- Memory: context windows, retrieval, and what "memory" actually means in a system that has none
- Orchestration and multi-step planning
- **Agentic loops and loop engineering** — the plan/act/observe cycle, why loops fail, and the engineering discipline required to keep them bounded, cheap and debuggable

**3. Evaluation as a first-class engineering concern**
Why "it looks good" is not a metric. Offline vs. online evaluation, golden datasets, LLM-as-judge and its failure modes, regression testing for non-deterministic systems, and how to know whether your change made things better.

**4. Explainability and governance — now board-level**
Explainable AI is back, and it arrived with regulation attached. What the current Indian regulatory picture means in practice, what auditability requires of your architecture, and why these decisions can no longer be deferred to a compliance team after launch.

**5. A live "wow" moment**
One real agent, one multi-step task, executed end to end in front of the room — research → synthesise → act. Not a video. Live, with the failure modes visible.

**6. The roadmap for the day**
Closing with exactly what's coming, so the workshop lands on prepared ground.

### 🔨 Build Your First AI Agent — Parts 1 & 2

**12:00 – 1:00 and 2:00 – 3:00 · 120 minutes total · Harsh Sahu**

The core of the day. Laptops open from minute one.

This is not a "follow along and copy the screen" workshop. You'll build incrementally, break things deliberately, and understand each layer before the next one goes on top.

**Part 1 (12:00 – 1:00) — From zero to a functioning loop**

- Environment setup and sanity check (done *before* the session via the pre-event guide — we won't burn workshop time on `pip install`)
- Your first model call, and what's actually happening in the request
- Adding a tool: giving the model the ability to *do* something
- The agentic loop: plan → act → observe → repeat
- Handling the first failures: malformed tool calls, infinite loops, runaway costs

Lunch break here is deliberate — it gives the concepts an hour to settle, and gives you an hour to ask the person next to you what they built.

**Part 2 (2:00 – 3:00) — From functioning to actually useful**

- Multi-step tasks and state management
- Memory: giving your agent something to work from
- Guardrails and bounded behaviour
- **Evaluating your agent** — writing your first eval, and watching a change make things measurably worse
- Deployment considerations: what changes when someone other than you runs it

**What you walk out with:**

- A working agent in a Git repository you own
- The full workshop codebase, documented, with extension paths
- An eval harness you can point at your next project
- A clear picture of what to build next

> **Bring:** a laptop, a charger, and your API credentials configured per the pre-event email. Power and Wi-Fi are provided, but download your dependencies beforehand — venue Wi-Fi with 150 people is a lesson nobody needs to learn twice.

## The Workshop: What You'll Actually Build

<a id="the-workshop"></a>

### The project

You will build an agent that takes a real, multi-step task and completes it end to end — planning its approach, calling tools to gather what it needs, holding state across steps, recovering when a step fails, and producing a verifiable result.

By the end of Part 2 it will be measurable: you'll have an evaluation harness pointed at it, and you'll be able to say whether a change made it better or worse rather than whether it *felt* better.

### Architecture you'll implement

```
   User intent
        │
        ▼
   ┌─────────────┐
   │   Planner   │◄──────────────┐
   └─────────────┘               │
        │                        │
        ▼                        │
   ┌─────────────┐         ┌─────────────┐
   │ Tool layer  │────────►│  Observer   │
   └─────────────┘         └─────────────┘
        │                        │
        ▼                        │
   ┌─────────────┐               │
   │   Memory    │───────────────┘
   └─────────────┘
        │
        ▼
   ┌─────────────┐        ┌─────────────┐
   │  Guardrails │───────►│    Evals    │
   └─────────────┘        └─────────────┘
        │
        ▼
     Result
```

Every box is something you build, not something you import.

### Pre-event setup

Ten days before the event, every ticket holder receives a setup guide:

- Environment and dependency installation
- API key acquisition and configuration
- A verification script — run it, get a green check, you're ready
- The starter repository
- A pre-event support channel in Discord, staffed for the week before

**Come with a working environment.** Two hours is enough to build something real, and not a minute more than that.

### Support during the workshop

Floating volunteers move through the room throughout both parts. If you're stuck, raise a hand — nobody spends fifteen minutes silently debugging an import error while the room moves on.

## Learning Outcomes

<a id="learning-outcomes"></a>

By 4 PM you will be able to:

**Understand**
- Explain the architectural difference between a chatbot and an agent, technically

- Implement an agent loop from scratch — plan, act, observe, repeat

- Write an evaluation for a non-deterministic system
- Build a small golden dataset

**Ship**
- Identify what changes between "runs on my laptop" and "runs for someone else"

## FAQ

<a id="faq"></a>

**Is this for beginners?**
It's for people comfortable with basic Python and a terminal. You don't need AI or ML experience — but you do need to be able to write and run code. If that's you, you'll be fine.

**Do I need to bring a laptop?**
Yes. Three hours of the day are hands-on. Bring a charger too.

**What if I can't set up my environment?**
You'll get a setup guide ten days early, plus a staffed support channel in Discord the week before. If you're still stuck, come thirty minutes early and a volunteer will sort it out.

**Do I need to pay for API access?**
You'll need working API credentials. The setup guide covers free-tier options that are sufficient for the workshop, and we'll provide fallback keys for anyone who hits a wall.

**Will sessions be recorded?**
Yes. Recordings and slides go to all attendees, and live in the `#recordings` and `#slides` channels.

**Is there a certificate?**
Yes, a certificate of participation. Though we'd gently suggest the repository you walk out with is more useful in an interview.

**Is this event only for students?**
No. It's calibrated for technically competent and curious people at any career stage — working engineers, founders, researchers and students all belong here. Students get discounted tickets; the content isn't watered down for anyone.

**Is the community free?**
Yes. Attendees get onboarded on the day; the community is open beyond that.

**Is it online or hybrid?**
In person only. The workshop, the AMA and the networking are the point, and none of them survive a webcam.

**Can my company sponsor or send a team?**
Yes to both. `sponsors@botmartz.com` for sponsorship, and team discounts start at three tickets.

**Will there be food options for dietary restrictions?**
Yes — vegetarian, non-vegetarian and Jain options. Mention anything else on the registration form.

**What's the refund policy?**
Detailed above. Short version: full refund early, transferable late.

**Will there be another edition?**
AI Frontier is annual. Quarterly mini-meetups run between editions, and the community runs weekly.
